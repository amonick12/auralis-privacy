Auralis Privacy Policy

Effective: October 22, 2026 App: Auralis (Auralis: Sound & Presence), iOS Developer: Aaron Monick ("we", "us") Contact: amonick12@gmail.com

Auralis is an adaptive audio companion. You speak, music plays, and Auralis listens and responds when it would help. We designed it so that your words, sessions and journal stay on your iPhone.

The short version

  • No account. Auralis doesn't ask who you are.
  • No tracking, no ads, no third-party analytics. Auralis has no analytics or advertising SDKs and never links your data with other companies' data. The only usage information we keep is on Auralis Cloud's relay, for subscribers: anonymous daily totals for the whole service, and today's counts and the month's estimated cost filed under a one-way hash of the subscription's id (see Auralis Cloud below); and, only if you say yes when Auralis asks, anonymous performance reports (crash and hang counts, launch time, memory and battery use, with no words and nothing that identifies you), added into daily totals by app version (see Performance reports below), and anonymous usage counts (how many times a day features were used, with no words, no names and nothing that identifies you), added into daily totals (see Usage counts below).
  • No session audio is ever recorded, stored or sent. The microphone is used for live, on-device transcription during a session, in Toning to find the note you're singing (on the device; only the current note is kept, in memory), and, if you keep music from another app playing, to recognize the song. The only audio Auralis keeps is a voice memo you choose to record for a journal entry, which stays with that entry.
  • Your sessions and journal are stored on your device, and in your own private iCloud only if you turn on iCloud sync. While you write an entry, a draft of it is kept on the device only (never in iCloud, and left out of backups) until you save, discard or burn it, so words aren't lost if the editor closes.
  • Your words leave your device only if you add your own key for an external AI provider, or subscribe to Auralis Cloud, and then only to that provider (or through Auralis's relay to its model service, which keep nothing), for the feature you're using. If you choose ChatGPT's styles for a journal picture, Image Playground also sends a few short phrases for it to ChatGPT, through Apple (see journal images below).

What Auralis stores on your device

Stored locally on your iPhone, not on our servers (we have none):

  • Sessions: transcripts of what you said, Auralis's replies, bookmarks, the music played, summaries and themes, your intention, and what the session worked with (a dream, a story's idea or a lesson's topic), with a link to the journal entry it came from when you chose one. Each session also keeps a short technical log with it: which AI model answered each reply and how long it took, the replies setting it started with, and when the screen locked or unlocked and the link to your Mac came or went. Since 18 October 2026 it also times each reply (from the end of your turn to the model, its first sentence and the voice's first sound, and which kind of voice spoke), notes how a reply was cut off and how much of it you heard (in characters), when you went on with a thought just after pausing, and how each of your turns ended (from the sound of your last word to the turn closing, the pause it waited for, whether your words sounded finished, and the language, and, with Smart Turn on, its verdict, roughly how sure it was and how long it took; the times of your words are kept only in memory, and no audio is stored), and how a reply from one of the smaller models (Apple's, the one you downloaded or your Mac's) was checked as it was said (whether a check left a sentence out or had it written again, and why, by name; whether the model was asked about it, and how long that took). It holds names and timings only (a model service's error by its status, type and code, never its message), and it is stored and synced exactly like the session it belongs to. A reply you spoke over or stopped is kept as far as you heard it. A session's saved setup holds neither your trusted contact nor the life portrait.
  • Journal: entries you write, import or add after sessions, and the tags you add to them.
  • Your photos and voice memos: photos you add to an entry (from your library or the camera, made smaller before they're kept) and voice memos you record for one. They stay with the entry, on your device and in your private iCloud if you turn on sync, and are never sent to any AI model. Choosing a photo uses Apple's photo picker, so Auralis sees only the photos you pick, not your library. If you ask Auralis to write a memo into the entry, it is transcribed on your device by Apple's speech recognition.
  • Preferences: settings such as voice, voice boost and talkativeness.
  • How you pause (since 18 October 2026): a few numbers per language, learned from your turns in sessions (how long you pause after a sentence before going on, and how often a turn closed while you were still going), so a turn ends when you've finished. Only on this device (not in iCloud), never sent, and never your words.
  • Music preferences: which kinds of music you skipped or listened through, used to choose better music.
  • What Patterns found in each entry: the words, names and places the on-device language tagger found, kept so each entry is read once. Only on this iPhone (not in iCloud); an entry's words are dropped once it's deleted, and all of them when you delete the journal. Which of those names are people, pets, public figures or you (sorted by your AI provider or your own devices' models, as described below, or set by you) is kept with your symbol notes, synced with your journal when sync is on, and deleted with the journal.
  • Meaning index (since 21 October 2026): numbers Apple's language model works out on your device from each journal entry and each session's summary, intention and marked moments, so related entries, Ask and Search can match what you mean as well as your words. Only on this device (not in iCloud, and left out of backups), never sent anywhere. An entry's numbers go once it's edited or deleted, all of them when you delete the journal, and they're worked out again when Apple updates its model. Where that model isn't on the device, Apple's older sentence embeddings are used instead, kept on the device in the same way.
  • Journal images: pictures you ask Auralis to make for an entry.
  • Recognized songs you choose to save.
  • Your week: the week's practice plan and the days you've ticked (the last eight weeks), on your device and synced with your settings through iCloud when sync is on.
  • Spoken audio cache: if you use an OpenAI or Auralis Cloud voice, the generated speech is kept on your device so the same line isn't generated twice.
  • Technical logs: audio, voice and song-recognition states (for example, "music resumed"), kept on your device. They never contain what was said.
  • Your AI API keys, if you add any: stored only in the iOS Keychain, never in the app's database or logs.
  • Widgets: if you add Auralis widgets to your Home Screen or Lock Screen, the app keeps a small summary for them on your device (in the Keychain shared with its widgets): the path you're on and its next day, your practice streak and minutes this week, and a quotation for each day. Widgets show it on your Home Screen and Lock Screen, where anyone holding your phone can see it. It never includes your journal or sessions' words, and a path made from your journal shows only as "Your path" while App Lock is on. It never leaves the device.

iCloud sync (optional, off by default). If you turn on Sync with iCloud in Settings, your sessions, journal (including pictures, your photos and voice memos) and settings are also kept in your private iCloud database, so they match across your devices, and your AI keys sync through iCloud Keychain. This uses your Apple Account under Apple's terms; we can't see it. iCloud Keychain is end-to-end encrypted; your sessions and journal are too if you turn on Advanced Data Protection for iCloud. Turning sync off keeps everything on the device.

App lock (optional, off by default). You can lock Auralis with Face ID, Touch ID or your passcode in Settings → Privacy & Safety. The check is done by iOS; Auralis never sees your face or passcode. When the lock is on, the app is covered in the app switcher, your journal is removed from Spotlight, Siri won't read it, and the morning dream reminder doesn't show the intention you set for your dreams. A session is never interrupted by the lock, but if the app was away longer than a minute during it (an overnight Sleep session), it asks again when the session ends.

You can delete individual sessions (History) and journal entries (Journal), delete all journal entries (Settings → Data & Storage), or delete everything by deleting the app. A session or entry you delete moves to Recently Deleted (the Journal's More menu, or Settings → Data & Storage), where you can restore it or delete it right away; after 30 days it is erased. Like other apps, this data may be included in your own iCloud or computer backup of your iPhone, under Apple's terms.

Sharing an entry or letter. Sharing an entry or a Looking Back letter as an image or PDF makes that file on your device, from that entry or letter only, and hands it to the share sheet; where it goes is up to you.

Microphone and speech

When a session is running and listening is on, Auralis uses the microphone to transcribe your speech on your device, using Apple's speech recognition. Audio is processed in memory and discarded. It is never recorded, saved or sent anywhere. You can turn listening off at any time during a session.

Toning. In the Toning meditation, the same microphone audio is also analysed on your device to find the pitch you're singing, so Auralis can hold a drone at that note. Only the current note is kept, in memory, while the session runs; no audio is recorded or sent, and sung vowels are not sent to any AI model.

Automatic speech. In Journaling's Automatic speech, what you said is saved to your journal word for word (it syncs like any entry if iCloud sync is on), and at the end that transcript is sent once to the AI provider chosen for the session, to pick a few lines to read back.

Voice memos. Outside sessions, when you tap Voice memo in a journal entry, Auralis asks for the microphone (the first time) and records until you tap stop. The memo is saved with that entry only, as described above, and never sent to any AI model.

Song recognition

If you keep music from another app playing during a session (for example YouTube or Spotify), Auralis can recognize the song using Apple's ShazamKit. A few seconds of microphone audio are turned on your device into a signature (a fingerprint of the music that can't be turned back into sound or speech), and only that signature is sent to Apple's Shazam service to find the song. Auralis doesn't listen for songs while it is speaking. Recognition is handled by Apple under Apple's privacy policy.

Artificial intelligence

Auralis generates replies in one of these ways, depending on your settings:

  1. On-device (Apple Intelligence): on supported iPhones, replies are generated on your device. Nothing is sent anywhere. On devices without Apple Intelligence, you can choose in Settings to download a private open model (Qwen3 1.7B or 4B on a phone, about 1 to 2.3 GB, from Hugging Face). The download contacts Hugging Face's servers for the model files only; no personal data is sent. Once downloaded, replies are generated on your device and nothing is sent anywhere. You can remove the model in Settings. Natural voices: Auralis downloads an open voice model (Kokoro, about 90 MB, from Hugging Face) by itself on Wi-Fi when your device speaks English and has room, or you can download it in Settings → Voice & Music. The download fetches the model files only; the voice is then made on your device and nothing you say or hear is sent anywhere. You can remove it in Settings, and then it isn't downloaded again unless you ask. Hearing when you've finished (experimental, since 18 October 2026): if you turn it on in Settings → Voice & Music, Auralis downloads Smart Turn, an open model (about 17 MB, from Hugging Face; the files only, no personal data is sent; kept out of your device's backups). On headphones only, while a session listens (never on the speaker, and never in Release), the last few seconds of the microphone's sound are held in memory for that model, on your device, to hear whether you've finished speaking. That sound is never stored or sent, and it's erased when the session stops listening or you turn the setting off. Your Mac: if you turn on sharing in Auralis on your Mac, your iPhone and iPad can use the Mac's larger downloaded model, at home or away. The devices connect directly to each other over an encrypted peer-to-peer connection (WebRTC); requests and replies go only between your devices. To set up the connection, each device writes its network addresses and a signed offer or answer to your private iCloud database, and asks public STUN servers (Google, Cloudflare) for its own public address; no conversation text passes through iCloud or any server. A shared secret in iCloud Keychain makes sure only your own devices can connect. On the Mac, a larger model can run in Auralis Model Host, a companion Mac app, which shares it with your iPhone and iPad the same way (your private iCloud for setup, the same iCloud Keychain secret, a direct encrypted connection); Auralis on the Mac can also pass it requests over the Mac's own loopback address, which nothing outside the Mac can reach. It does so only after Auralis Model Host proves it holds the same iCloud Keychain secret, and each request and reply is encrypted with it, so no other program on the Mac can read them.
  2. Private Cloud Compute: not used. Auralis will offer it, off by default, once Apple approves it for the app; then requests may be processed by Apple's Private Cloud Compute, which Apple designed so your data is not retained or accessible to Apple. Never while replies are Private, or for a session set to Private: then Apple's model runs only on your device.
  3. A cloud AI service with your own key (optional): if you add your own API key for a provider (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google Gemini, or a server you set up), the text of the current conversation, and, in Journaling, Custom, Record a Dream, Dreamwork, Active Imagination and Arcana Reading sessions, a few passages from your past sessions or journal that are relevant to what you just said, is sent to that provider to generate a reply. Now and then a Journaling, Custom, Inquiry or Dreamwork session opens by picking up something from your last two weeks (Settings → Journal & Reminders → "Pick up where we left off", on unless you turn it off): the one sentence you wrote or said about it, or a dream image, goes to that provider to word the opening line (with no key, your Mac's model words it, or a set line is used and nothing is sent). Which ones were picked up is kept on this device. If you've added keys for more than one and the one you chose is unavailable, Auralis may use another one you added. If you've turned on any traditions (Traditions, at the bottom of Practice), their names and a sentence about each go with the request (replies, Looking Back, the briefing for readings), so they can use their terms. In Dreamwork, Active Imagination, Arcana, Integration, Learning and a tradition's practices, a few short passages from the Library's own courses (Auralis's text, chosen on your device by matching what you said) go with the request too; nothing of yours is added to them. Tradition reminders are scheduled on your device; nothing about them is sent anywhere. When you ask a question about your past sessions or journal (in Search, with Ask Auralis on Today, the Journal, Siri, Control Center or the widget, or by voice in a session), the question and the passages that match it are sent to check the matches and write the answer; nothing else from your journal goes with them. The questions offered under "Ask about your journal" are written by the same AI that answers it (your own devices when replies are Private), from short excerpts of your last entries and the themes, dream images and names Patterns found, sent when the questions are first shown after Auralis opens, then again only on a new day or when your recent entries change. A question about sleep, heart or rest is answered with Apple Health too: when your own Mac's model or a model on your device writes the answer, it's given your last four weeks of nightly sleep hours, resting heart rate and heart rate variability, and the patterns Auralis found. A cloud AI service is never given those numbers; it gets only the pattern sentences, and only if you turn on Include health patterns with cloud AI. Siri and Shortcuts open the answer only on an unlocked device, and the app's own Face ID lock still applies. When you ask Practice to make a path from your journal, the theme you chose and up to six entries about it are sent to write the days. The title and artist of the song playing are included so replies can refer to it, and the local time of day, so a question about the time can be answered. In the Journal, features you start yourself send the entries they work on: reflecting on an entry sends that entry and up to three earlier ones; a symbol's thread sends the entries that mention it; Looking Back sends every entry in the span you chose (a week, a month or three months), and, if Write the weekly letter by itself is on (Settings → Journal & Reminders; on if you've had a letter written before), the past week's entries once a week when there are three or more, only to Auralis Cloud, your own key's provider or your Mac; adding a session to your journal (and, when it ends, a Record a Dream or Integration session, unless you turned journal entries after sessions off) sends what you said in it (or, if you said little, its notes) to be made into an entry in your own words; proofreading an entry sends that entry. Without being asked, Auralis also tags your journal: it sends each entry's opening lines (its first 320 characters) to tell dreams from other entries, and its first 700 characters to find its themes, once (again if you edit it); and each new name it finds, with up to three sentences it appears in (each up to 160 characters, from your entries or a session's intention, summary, marked moments or what it worked with), to sort which names are people, pets, public figures or you. An update that changes how Auralis tags may send them again. This goes to your provider or Auralis Cloud only while you're online and replies aren't Private; offline, with Private, or with no key or Auralis Cloud in use, your own devices' models (your Mac's, Apple's or the one you downloaded) do it, and nothing is sent. When enough new words recur, the list of your journal's recurring words (single words and names, no sentences) is sent to group the words for one thing (snake and serpent): only to your provider's strongest model or Auralis Cloud's while you're online and replies aren't Private, otherwise your Mac's large model, never a model on this device. That data is handled under the provider's own terms and privacy policy. In Storytelling and Learning, the topic you give is sent too; if you choose to continue a dream as a story, that dream's text is sent as the topic. In Dreamwork, the dream you choose is sent so the replies can work with it. Auralis never sends audio.
  4. Built-in responses: without any of the above, Auralis uses fixed, on-device responses and sends nothing.

Which one answers. With replies set to Private (Settings → AI & Models), only the on-device models (1) answer, and nothing goes to a service: for every session, however it starts (Start again, Your week, a path, a widget or the Watch), with a voice on your device speaking it and reading aloud, and with a picture's scene described on your device (only that description goes out to be painted, when you ask for a picture in one of the services' styles; in one of Image Playground's own, it's painted on your device and nothing goes out; in ChatGPT's, only its short phrases go, through Apple, as described under journal images below). A session you set to Private in its setup while Settings is Automatic or Cloud stays Private after it too: its summary if it's written later (after the app closed mid-session), what you said in it when you add it to your journal, the questions suggested for a reading set to Private, and its summary when a later day of a path recalls it (left out of anything that may go to a service). With Cloud, only the cloud services answer, your provider (3) or Auralis Cloud (offline there are built-in responses, and an Arcana reading goes on with your own devices). With Automatic, Auralis uses the best available: your provider (3) or Auralis Cloud first, if you've added a key or subscribe and are online, then your Mac's model, then Apple's on-device model, then this device's downloaded model, then built-in responses. So with Automatic and a key, conversations are sent to your provider whenever you're online; choose Private to keep them on your devices.

Replies started early. With your own key, Auralis Cloud or your Mac's model, a reply may be started a moment before your turn closes, once what you've said sounds finished (since 18 October 2026): the same conversation is sent, with your words so far. If you go on, or something else should come first (a command, a safety check-in), it is cancelled, and nothing of it is said, shown or kept; with Auralis Cloud it still counts toward the day's requests, so a session stops starting them once most go unused. A model on your device is never asked early.

Safety check. When something you say in a session sounds heavy (a few words checked on your device first), the same model that answers is also asked whether it might be a wish not to exist: it gets what you just said (from a long stretch of talk, only its heavier sentences and the one before each) and the two lines before it, nothing else. Its one-word answer can bring up Need Help; it is kept only while the session runs and is never saved or included in exports or problem reports.

Auralis Cloud (optional subscription). If you subscribe to Auralis Cloud and it is the service in use (replies Automatic or Cloud), the request a reply needs — the same text an external provider would receive — goes over HTTPS to Auralis's relay, a small program on Cloudflare Workers, which passes it to the company that runs the open model (now Together AI, running DeepSeek V4.1 Flash for replies, and a larger open model such as DeepSeek V4 Pro for reading across your whole journal, up to 30 times a day; named in Settings → AI & Models → Auralis Cloud) and streams the answer back. If that company's service is down or too slow, the relay asks a backup instead: an open model run by Cloudflare Workers AI on Cloudflare's network (the same company that runs the relay), which receives the same request (shortened to fit that model if it's very long) and keeps neither it nor the answer. With the request, the app sends the App Store's signed record of your subscription; the relay asks Apple whether it is active and never learns your name or Apple Account. The relay does not log or store what you say or what the model answers, nor the model service's error messages: it keeps only a flag saying the subscription is active (for up to a day, checked with Apple again after an hour), counts of today's use (requests, journal reads, pictures, characters spoken; for up to two days) and the month's estimated cost of your use and a mark that the subscription was used that month (until the month is over), all filed under a one-way hash of the subscription's id. The month's figure is how Auralis Cloud keeps each subscription within a monthly allowance: past most of it, journal reads use the everyday model and pictures pause; past all of it, Auralis Cloud pauses until the 1st. It also keeps anonymous daily totals for running the service (how many replies, voice lines, pictures and journal reads were asked for that day, how many failed and with which status, and which model answered, and what that used: tokens, characters and pictures, and their estimated cost; how long replies took to their first words and to their end, by model and by feature, and how long voice lines took to start, in a few time ranges; how many subscriptions came near a daily limit or the monthly allowance; how many subscriptions used it that day and that month; and, per feature, how many requests and their estimated cost). Each request the app sends to Auralis Cloud names the feature that sent it, as one fixed word from a list (for example "lookingBack", "journalTagging" or "voice"), so these totals can say what the requests were for; the word is counted with the day's anonymous totals and kept nowhere else. These totals have nothing in them about who asked or what was said; they're kept up to 120 days. For the same reason the relay keeps its last 50 failures: when, which kind of request (a reply, a voice line, a picture, Apple's check), the status, the model and a short error code; nothing about who asked or what was said, and not the services' error messages. To spot unusually heavy use, a subscription's estimated cost for the day and the month is kept with its other counts under the same hash, and a hash whose day or month passes another 25 cents is listed with that figure (the day's for eight days, the month's for forty); nothing else about you goes with it. The developer sees these totals and lists on a private page only they can open. From that page the developer can pause Auralis Cloud for one subscription (for example, one being misused): only its hash and the date are kept, its requests are then refused before anything is sent on (the app says so and your other models answer), and the day's totals count pauses and refused requests as numbers. That page also shows App Store Connect's own aggregate reports, which the relay fetches once a day with the developer's App Store Connect key: downloads, active subscriptions by product, subscription events, installs and deletions, devices, sessions and crashes (these last from people who chose to share analytics with app developers, and only where at least five people are counted), and the number of TestFlight testers and each recent build's installs, sessions and crashes. Only the daily numbers are kept (for 35 days); Apple's reports name no one, and nothing in them is linked to your subscription's hash or anything you said. When something fails or slows down, or a build of the app crashes more than before, the developer gets an alert with counts, times, status codes, model names and app versions only (for a crash, also its kind and its place in the app's code, from the anonymous performance reports below). Apple also tells the relay when a subscription is refunded, renewed or lapses (its signed notice is checked as the app's is), so the "active" flag is updated at once; only the notice's type is logged. The developer is told that someone subscribed or a subscription ended, and gets a short morning summary of the day before in counts (requests, failures, estimated cost, how many subscriptions used it, subscriptions started and ended, App Store downloads and active subscriptions, crashes and hangs by app version, how many anonymous reports came in, the most used features and how many finished or skipped the first-launch pages), never who. The model service is used under a contract term that it keeps no prompts or answers and trains on none. If your sessions use an Auralis Cloud voice, each line Auralis is about to say is sent the same way, through the relay to the same company's open voice model (Kokoro), with the voice you chose (if that voice is down, Cloudflare Workers AI's voice model speaks the line instead, on Cloudflare's network); the audio comes back and is kept only in this device's cache. When the voice you chose is also one of the natural voices on your device (Heart, Bella, Nicole or Michael, once they're downloaded, in English), a line is made on your device instead and isn't sent, with the screen on or locked: a line is sent only before the voice on your device is ready, or for the rest of a line it couldn't finish. Reading aloud works the same way. The relay keeps a count of characters spoken today, under the same hash, and nothing else. Cancelling the subscription stops all of this; Private keeps everything on your devices, voices and reading aloud included.

Paths that remember. A day of a path builds on the days before: the short summaries of the sessions that completed its earlier days go with that day's session to the model that answers, and to the same model once while you set it up, to write a line linking them to today. Nothing else from those sessions is sent.

Photos of tarot cards (Arcana Reading) If you photograph your cards or choose a photo of them for Arcana Reading, Auralis reads the card names on your device with Apple's text recognition and keeps only which cards you drew. Your photo is compared with the reference cards on your device; it isn't stored or sent anywhere. The cards, their traditional meanings and your question are part of the reading, sent to an AI provider only as described above. So the reading can be about your life, it also includes, while "Journal informs conversations" is on (Settings → Journal & Reminders): a few lines from each of your last several journal entries and recent dreams (from the past three weeks), your journal's recurring themes this month, your last session's summary and your path day, and a private portrait of your life (the same, without the portrait, when you ask setup to suggest questions): a briefing on the experiences, people, places, ideas and dreams in your journal, which a model writes from your whole journal when you first set up a reading and then updates with your new entries. The portrait is kept on your device (not with your sessions, and not in iCloud), and deleted with your journal; a line of it resting on an entry you've deleted isn't used. Writing or updating it sends your journal (or its new entries) to the model doing the writing: your AI provider's strongest model if you've added a key and chose Automatic or Cloud, otherwise your Mac's model. It is never written by a model on this device, which isn't accurate enough for it. A second pass by the same model checks the portrait against your journal, line by line, and removes anything the journal doesn't support (so your journal goes to that model twice). Each line quotes the words of the entry it comes from; your device then drops any line whose words aren't found in your journal (this check stays on the device). The same goes for Looking Back's letters, the threads of a symbol or a dream series, reading your dreams for series (and checking the series found by shared images, and "Add a series"), and writing a personal path. Without a key or your Mac, those wait until one is available. Reading your dreams for series, and checking those series, happen without being asked (once when you have enough dreams, and when you open your series): every dream's text goes to that model, never to a model on this device. Opening a series found by shared images that has no name yet sends the start of each of its dreams (up to 500 characters each) and the shared images to name it: to your key's provider's or Auralis Cloud's everyday model while you're online and replies aren't Private, otherwise to your own devices' models.

Release (a kind of journaling, and "Let this one burn" for a new written entry) keeps only that it happened: its kind, when it started and ended, its length, your arriving and leaving check-ins, and a timeline without any text (when the bells rang and the page burned). Never your words or Auralis's, never a journal entry (whatever the journal is set to), nothing for Spotlight, Siri, the Live Activity or the Watch beyond its name, and nothing that could bring the words back if the app closes during it. No AI model hears them; the one exception is safety's second look at something heavy the phrases miss, and only on a model on your device (Apple's own, or one you downloaded), never a cloud service or your Mac. The words are held in memory until they burn. A new written entry you let burn was kept as a draft on your device while you wrote it, as any entry is; the draft is deleted the moment you let it burn.

The week in review (a kind of journaling) tells the model what it tells you at the start: how many sessions you finished in the last seven days and their names (the practice, or a flow's name). No dates, times or what was said in them. A recurring feeling sends the feeling you typed, if any, with your words, as journaling does.

Planning Your week for you sends the same model the names of the practices you did in the last month and how often, your journal's recurring themes as single words (not your entries), the traditions you've turned on, what you type about the week, which practices your own check-ins show usually leave you calmer or with more energy (their names and how often, never the check-ins themselves), and, when you've done one in the last week, the summary of your week in review. Which practices help you is worked out on your device from your check-ins and isn't kept or sent anywhere else. Nothing is sent until you ask for a week, and a balanced week needs no model at all.

The Library (an optional tab) keeps its courses and your progress on your device, synced through iCloud with your settings when sync is on (a lesson written out in full stays on the device that wrote it). When you add a topic, what you typed and the names of the traditions you've turned on go to the same model (the strongest hosted model with your key, else your Mac's) to write its course. Going deeper into a finished course, writing a tradition's course afresh, or writing a part out to read sends the course's outline (not your journal) to that model, and fact-checking a written course sends its outline to the same model. "Suggest more for me", and "Generate more" on a shelf's page, send your traditions and the themes of your sessions and journal (single words, not your entries), with the names of the Library's own courses and earlier suggestions so that what comes back is new, never the name of a topic you added; they and a part's recall questions (which send that part's text, not your journal) go to Auralis Cloud's or your key's everyday model while online, unless replies are Private, otherwise to your own devices' models. A Library course or story is taught in a Learning or Storytelling session like any other. With an OpenAI key or an Auralis Cloud subscription and "Pictures drawn for posts" on, a few posts a day get a picture drawn (by OpenAI's image model with your key, else by Auralis Cloud's open one): only that post's short title and first words are sent (never a recall question, never anything from your journal), and the picture is kept on your device. The same portrait (shortened for a model on your own devices) also goes with each request in conversation sessions (Journaling, Dream sessions, Active Imagination, Inquiry, Custom, and Psychedelic Support when exploring ideas), so Auralis understands who you are; with a key, starting one of those sessions may update it in the background. Turn "Journal informs conversations" off to keep all of this out.

OpenAI voices and journal images (optional)

So there's no silence between sentences, the next sentence of a reply is sent to the voice service you chose (OpenAI or Auralis Cloud) while the one before it is being said. If you speak over Auralis or stop it, that sentence may have been sent and never spoken. In the same way, a guided practice's next one or two scripted lines are sent before they're due, so they play without waiting on the network; a line with your own words in it (an intention, a dream sign) is sent only when it's said. If the session ends first, those lines may have been sent and never spoken. With an Auralis Cloud voice that's also on your device, these are sent only while lines would be said from Auralis Cloud (the voice on your device not ready yet); if it stops being ready, the next sentence and the practice's next lines are sent at once.

If you add an OpenAI API key and choose an OpenAI voice (and replies aren't Private), the text Auralis is about to speak is sent to OpenAI to turn it into speech: Auralis's own replies and guidance, and any text you ask it to read aloud, such as a journal entry or text you share to Auralis from another app. Your voice is never sent.

If you ask Auralis to make an image for a journal entry, the entry's text is sent to your AI provider (above) to describe a scene (with Private, a model on your own devices describes it, so the entry doesn't leave them), and only that scene description is sent to paint the picture: to OpenAI with your key, or, without one (or when its credits run out), to Auralis Cloud, whose relay passes it to an image model run by Cloudflare Workers AI on Cloudflare's network, the same company that runs the relay (Leonardo's Lucid Origin or Phoenix, or the open FLUX.1 [schnell]; Together AI only if those fail) and keeps neither the description nor the picture; a subscriber can make up to 20 pictures a day. The image is stored with the entry on your device.

Both are handled under OpenAI's terms and privacy policy.

On a device with Apple Intelligence you can also choose one of Image Playground's styles (Illustration, Sketch, Animation). Apple's Image Playground then paints the picture on your device from the scene description, and neither the description nor the picture is sent anywhere to be painted: its sheet opens with the scene, and you keep the picture or close it. The scene is still described as above, and the same model turns it into a few short phrases for Image Playground: with Automatic or Cloud by your AI provider (the entry's text, then the scene, go to it, as for any picture), with Private on your own devices, so nothing leaves them. When neither OpenAI nor Auralis Cloud can paint a picture you asked for in one of their styles (no key, out of credits, today's pictures used, paused or failing), Image Playground on your device paints it instead, in the nearest of its own styles (never ChatGPT's). ChatGPT's styles in Image Playground (oil painting, watercolor and others, chosen in its sheet) are a separate choice, last in the picture menu under "Sends the phrases to ChatGPT, through Apple", and never made for you: only when you pick it does Image Playground send the picture's short phrases (the scene itself if none could be written), and anything you add in its sheet, to OpenAI's ChatGPT through Apple's ChatGPT integration, under Apple's and OpenAI's terms, as Apple's ChatGPT setting describes.

Performance reports (optional, off until you say yes)

About once a day iOS gives Auralis a report of how it ran on your device (MetricKit). With Settings → Privacy & Safety → Share anonymous reports on, which it is only if you say yes, Auralis reduces each report on your device to a small summary and sends it to Auralis Cloud's relay, whether or not you subscribe. The summary holds only: the app's version and build, the iOS version (like 27.0), the device model (like iPhone17,1), whether it's a TestFlight or App Store build, and numbers: how long launches and resumes took and how long hangs lasted (in a few ranges), CPU and GPU time, time on screen and in the background, peak memory, disk writes, data used over Wi-Fi and cellular, location use, and how many times the app ended and why (closed normally, ran out of memory, crashed, too slow). For a crash or a hang it holds the kind of problem (the exception and signal) and one place in Auralis's own code (a position in the app's binary, like "Auralis+0x1a2b3c"), nothing else of the report: no other code, no file paths or names, nothing you said or wrote, no journal, no account, subscription or device identifier. It's sent without your subscription's token. Development builds never send.

The relay checks every field, refuses anything else, and doesn't keep the report: it adds it into daily totals by app version (and, for launch time and memory, by device model), which only the developer sees and which are deleted after 35 days. Like any connection, the request carries your network address; the relay uses it only to limit how often one address can send (at most 10 a minute) and doesn't keep it. A report that can't be sent waits on your device and is tried again when you next open Auralis; turning the switch off deletes any waiting and sends nothing more. The full reports stay on your device, as before.

Asking you first

Auralis asks whether to share anonymous reports when you first open it, on its privacy page, with neither answer chosen; if you had Auralis before it asked, a small card on Today asks once instead. Until you answer, and after a no, nothing is counted or sent: not the reports, not the counts below, and not your answer. A yes on that page also counts which first-launch pages you'd seen before it, as the counts below describe. You can change it any time in Settings → Privacy & Safety; with iCloud sync on, the answer is the same on your other devices.

Usage counts (optional, off until you say yes)

The same switch, Settings → Privacy & Safety → Share anonymous reports, also sends a day's counts of what was used in the app, to help make Auralis better. During the day Auralis counts on your device how many times certain things happened, from a fixed list, and nothing else: sessions started (by kind, like Meditation or Sleep), how long they ran (in a few ranges: under 5 minutes, 5–15, 15–30, 30–60, an hour or more) and whether they ended early; journal entries saved (typed, spoken, or from a session) and dreams recorded; Library lessons opened and finished; path days finished; Looking Back letters; Ask questions asked; journal pictures made; on the first-launch pages, which pages were reached, where the rest was skipped, and the choices made there (how to begin, and which kind of first session Begin opened, like a breath sit or a dream told; versions before October 21, 2026 also counted how many traditions were picked, never which, whether the Library was left on, and Automatic or Private replies), and whether your yes to these reports was given there or later; problems (the cloud voice handing a line to another voice and why, in a few kinds; a session's problems, like music that wouldn't start; replies a model couldn't give, by the kind of model; how many times a safety check-in was asked or Need Help opened, a number only); and on Auralis Cloud's page, whether it was opened (and from where: Settings, the first-launch pages, or a screen offering it because nothing on your devices could do that work, along with how often such an offer was shown) and a purchase started, finished, cancelled, failed or restored.

The counts never hold anything you said or wrote, the name of anything you made (an entry, a dream, a topic, a path), your traditions' practices, a time finer than the day, or anything that identifies you or your device. The day after, the next time you open Auralis or leave it, the day's counts go to Auralis Cloud's relay with the app's version and build, the iOS major version (like 27) and whether it's a TestFlight or App Store build, without your subscription's token and without your device model, whether or not you subscribe. Development builds never send.

The relay accepts only the names on its own copy of the list, limits every count, and doesn't keep the report: it adds the counts into daily totals for everyone together (the first-launch counts also by app version), which only the developer sees and which are deleted after 35 days. As with performance reports, your network address is used only to limit how often one address can send and isn't kept. Turning the switch off deletes the day's counts and anything waiting to be sent, and nothing more is counted.

Auralis Cloud's page also asks the relay how much of today's allowance you've used. That request carries only your subscription's signed transaction; the relay answers from the counts it already keeps for its daily limits and stores nothing new.

Apple Health (optional, off by default)

If you turn on Log to Apple Health in Settings (or say yes when the review of your first Meditation, Breathwork, Shamanic Journey, Album Journey or Inquiry session offers it, which logs that session too), Auralis writes these entries to the Health app on your iPhone: Mindful Minutes for finished Meditation, Breathwork, Shamanic Journey, Album Journey and Inquiry sessions; a workout for each finished Movement session (its kind, such as walking, yoga or tai chi, and its start and end time, nothing else: no route, heart rate or calories; Apple's Health sheet asks for this as your first Movement session starts), or, when your Apple Watch with Auralis records it, the Watch's workout instead (see Apple Watch below), never both; and State of Mind from your arriving and leaving check-ins.

Separately, only if you turn on Show sleep and heart patterns, Auralis reads your sleep, resting heart rate, heart rate variability and the mindful minutes other apps logged, and works out on your iPhone how they go with your sessions, your check-ins, your journal's themes and the dreams you record (for example, how you slept on nights after an evening session). The patterns are shown on Today and in Noticing. Your Health data itself is never sent to a cloud AI service, to iCloud or to us; the only model that ever sees its numbers is your own Mac's (over Auralis's link) or one on your device, when you ask about sleep or heart.

A Looking Back letter reads only your journal: it's never given these patterns or anything else from Health. You can revoke or change any of this at any time in the Health app or in Auralis's settings.

After a Sleep session, the session shows how long it took you to fall asleep: Auralis asks once (with Apple's Health sheet) to read sleep, and finds when the night's sleep began after the session started. This is read on your iPhone in the same way, kept only on your iPhone (not in iCloud), and never leaves it. When your Apple Watch watched the session for sleep (below), the next morning Auralis compares when it noticed you fall asleep (or that it didn't) with when Health's record says you did, on your iPhone, and uses the difference only to tune when the Watch notices sleep for you. For each of the last 60 such nights, the times involved (when the session began, when the Watch noticed sleep, when Health says it began, when you last spoke in the session) and that setting are kept in a file on your iPhone (not in iCloud or device backups), never sent anywhere, and deleted with Delete All Sessions.

If you turn on Play a chime while you dream (Wake back to bed, or Dream incubation aiming for a lucid dream), Auralis reads your sleep stages from the last month, with the same permission, to work out when REM sleep usually comes for you, and plays the chime then. The stages are read on your iPhone when the session starts, used only for that night's timing, and neither kept nor sent anywhere. Nothing is recorded during the night.

Your week. They are read on your iPhone, never kept, and never sent anywhere.

Lucid dreaming. A lucid night's session names up to three of them aloud before you sleep, so that one line is part of the session like the rest of its guidance: kept in its transcript (synced with your sessions when sync is on), read by the model that sums the session up, and spoken by the voice you chose. Reality checks are local notifications scheduled on your iPhone (on or off in Lucid dreaming, or Settings → Journal & Reminders). If you ask Auralis to wake you for Wake back to bed, it sets an alarm with Apple's alarm system for that one time.

Apple Watch. If you have the Auralis Watch app, it follows a session started on your iPhone: it runs a mindfulness workout to read your heart rate, shows it, and sends it to the iPhone, where the session can ease the music toward calm when your heart rate climbs. The workout isn't saved to Health, except in Movement (below). About once a minute a reading is kept for the session's review, on that iPhone only, until the session is erased: it isn't part of the session's record, so it never goes to iCloud, even with sync on, and is left out of device backups (the session's log notes only that a reading came, without the number). It's never sent to an AI provider. You can turn the Watch off for sessions, or just the music easing, in Settings → Health & Devices.

The Watch app also has breathing sessions of its own. The first time you start one, the Watch asks (with Apple's Health sheet) to run a mindfulness workout, read heart rate and save Mindful Minutes. During the session the workout keeps it running with your wrist down and shows your heart rate on the Watch; the workout is then discarded, and if you allowed it the session is saved to Health as Mindful Minutes (its start and end time, nothing else). The heart rate stays on the Watch and isn't kept. The Watch keeps a count of today's breathing minutes for its own screen and complications.

Movement on the Watch. With Log to Apple Health on, a Movement session on your iPhone is recorded by the Watch when it's there: it runs a workout of the practice's kind (walking, yoga, tai chi and so on) and saves it to Health on the Watch with its start and end, the heart rate and active energy the Watch measured, as Apple's Workout app would; the iPhone then saves no workout of its own. If the Watch isn't there or can't confirm in time, the iPhone saves its workout as above. You can also start Movement on the Watch: the first time, Apple's Health sheet asks to save workouts and read heart rate and active energy. The Watch asks your iPhone to begin the guided session; if your iPhone isn't in reach it's a timed workout on the Watch, saved to Health the same way unless Log to Apple Health is off on your iPhone. These readings stay in Health on your devices; Auralis doesn't send them anywhere.

Your iPhone and Watch talk to each other directly over Apple's WatchConnectivity (the same private link Apple uses between paired devices; Auralis has no server in between). The iPhone sends the Watch the running session's name, part and time left (and in Movement the line being said, from the practice's script, and a request to record the workout), and, for the Watch's home screen and complications, today's path day (the path's name and the day's title), the day's quotation from the Library, today's practice minutes and whether Log to Apple Health is on. With App Lock on, a path made from your journal is sent as "Your path", without its day's title, as the iPhone's widgets show it. The Watch sends the iPhone your heart rate during a session, and your requests to start a Meditation, Sleep, Breathwork, Integration or Movement session there or to pause, resume or end the running one, and whether it has recorded a Movement workout (or, if it couldn't save one, its kind and times, for the iPhone to save instead). None of it goes anywhere else.

The iPhone also sends the Watch the timing of the next minute or so of breaths and holds (so the Watch can tap them), your usual bedtime and whether yesterday was a Psychedelic Support session (so the Smart Stack can offer Sleep or Integration at the right time). While the Need Help button is on the iPhone's screen, it sends the fixed lines with 988 (when Need Help offers it) or the emergency number, so the Watch can offer them and Ground Me; never your trusted contact. If you ask for Wake back to bed's alarm on your Watch, it sends the alarm's time, and the Watch tells it when the alarm is set (and when you say you're awake). From the Watch you can:

  • Record a dream. What you dictate goes to your iPhone and is saved in your journal as a dream, like a dream written in the morning notification. The Watch keeps the first few words of a dream only until your iPhone says it's in your journal (at most ten waiting).
  • Check in at the start or end of a session. Your calm and energy are saved with that session and, if you allowed it, logged to Health as State of Mind, as on the iPhone. The Watch asks the iPhone whether a check-in it shows is still open (just yes or no).
  • Ask Auralis. Your question goes to your iPhone, which answers it from your journal and sessions with the AI you chose, exactly as Ask Auralis does on the iPhone (see the AI section), and sends the answer back. While App Lock is on, the iPhone doesn't answer.

Heart rate and stillness guiding a session (both in Settings → Health & Devices). "Heart rate guides the session" is off unless you turn it on: in Breathwork your heart rate can gently slow the breath, and in Psychedelic Support a sharp, lasting rise can bring an offer to come back to the breath. "Notice when I fall asleep" (on by default) uses your heart rate and, during a Sleep session only, how still your wrist is (the Watch's motion sensor; it asks permission first) to let the session end sooner when you seem asleep (nothing more is said, the microphone goes off and the music fades out), and keeps that time on your iPhone (not in iCloud) so the session's review can show it and the next morning can compare it with Health (above). Anything you said in the session in the ten minutes before means you were awake: only that you spoke is used, from what the session already heard. The motion readings are reduced on the Watch to a single "how still" number every half minute, sent to your iPhone and not kept. Heart rate and motion stay on your Watch and iPhone and are never sent to an AI provider.

Music

If you connect Apple Music, Auralis uses Apple's MusicKit to play music and read your library or playlists for soundtrack choices. Playback and your Apple Music account are handled by Apple under Apple's privacy policy. Auralis does not share your sessions or journal with Apple Music. If you keep music from another app playing, Auralis reads only what the system reports as playing (title and artist), and can pause or resume it when you ask.

Each song in a session's review links to Apple Music. For a song that played from your own library or was recognized by ShazamKit, Auralis may look it up once in the Apple Music catalog by its title and artist to link its page; what it finds is kept on your device. Nothing else about the session is sent.

Safety features

During a session, Auralis can offer to call a trusted contact you entered, or emergency services. Nothing is ever dialed or sent without you tapping to place the call, and your trusted contact is stored only on your device (not in iCloud, not with your sessions, and never in a feedback report). Auralis is not a medical device and does not provide medical advice.

Children

Auralis is rated 18+ on the App Store and is meant for adults; it is not directed to children, and we don't knowingly collect information from anyone under 18. What we receive at all is limited to what's described above: Auralis Cloud's anonymous daily totals and the counts filed under a hashed subscription id, anonymous performance reports and usage counts (only if you said yes), a support email you choose to send, and beta feedback a tester chooses to send.
