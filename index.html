Auralis Privacy Policy

Effective: October 13, 2026 App: Auralis (Auralis: Sound & Presence), iOS Developer: Aaron Monick ("we", "us") Contact: amonick12@gmail.com

Auralis is an adaptive audio companion. You speak, music plays, and Auralis listens and responds when it would help. We designed it so that your words, sessions and journal stay on your iPhone.

The short version

  • No account. Auralis doesn't ask who you are.
  • No tracking, no ads, no third-party analytics. Auralis has no analytics or advertising SDKs and never links your data with other companies' data. The only usage information we keep is on Auralis Cloud's relay, for subscribers: anonymous daily totals for the whole service, and today's counts filed under a one-way hash of the subscription's id (see Auralis Cloud below).
  • No session audio is ever recorded, stored or sent. The microphone is used for live, on-device transcription during a session, in Toning to find the note you're singing (on the device; only the current note is kept, in memory), and, if you keep music from another app playing, to recognize the song. The only audio Auralis keeps is a voice memo you choose to record for a journal entry, which stays with that entry.
  • Your sessions and journal are stored on your device, and in your own private iCloud only if you turn on iCloud sync.
  • Your words leave your device only if you add your own key for an external AI provider, or subscribe to Auralis Cloud, and then only to that provider (or through Auralis's relay to its model service, which keep nothing), for the feature you're using.

What Auralis stores on your device

Stored locally on your iPhone, not on our servers (we have none):

  • Sessions: transcripts of what you said, Auralis's replies, bookmarks, the music played, summaries and themes. Each session also keeps a short technical log with it: which AI model answered each reply and how long it took, the replies setting it started with, and when the screen locked or unlocked and the link to your Mac came or went. It holds names and timings only, and it is stored and synced exactly like the session it belongs to.
  • Journal: entries you write, import or add after sessions, and the tags you add to them.
  • Your photos and voice memos: photos you add to an entry (from your library or the camera, made smaller before they're kept) and voice memos you record for one. They stay with the entry, on your device and in your private iCloud if you turn on sync, and are never sent to any AI model. Choosing a photo uses Apple's photo picker, so Auralis sees only the photos you pick, not your library. If you ask Auralis to write a memo into the entry, it is transcribed on your device by Apple's speech recognition.
  • Preferences: settings such as voice, voice boost and talkativeness.
  • Music preferences: which kinds of music you skipped or listened through, used to choose better music.
  • Journal images: pictures you ask Auralis to make for an entry.
  • Recognized songs you choose to save.
  • Spoken audio cache: if you use an OpenAI or Auralis Cloud voice, the generated speech is kept on your device so the same line isn't generated twice.
  • Technical logs: audio, voice and song-recognition states (for example, "music resumed"), kept on your device. They never contain what was said.
  • Your AI API keys, if you add any: stored only in the iOS Keychain, never in the app's database or logs.
  • Widgets: if you add Auralis widgets to your Home Screen or Lock Screen, the app keeps a small summary for them on your device (in the Keychain shared with its widgets): the path you're on and its next day, your practice streak and minutes this week, and a quotation for each day. Widgets show it on your Home Screen and Lock Screen, where anyone holding your phone can see it. It never includes your journal or sessions' words, and a path made from your journal shows only as "Your path" while App Lock is on. It never leaves the device.

iCloud sync (optional, off by default). If you turn on Sync with iCloud in Settings, your sessions, journal (including pictures, your photos and voice memos) and settings are also kept in your private iCloud database, so they match across your devices, and your AI keys sync through iCloud Keychain. This uses your Apple Account under Apple's terms; we can't see it. iCloud Keychain is end-to-end encrypted; your sessions and journal are too if you turn on Advanced Data Protection for iCloud. Turning sync off keeps everything on the device.

App lock (optional, off by default). You can lock Auralis with Face ID, Touch ID or your passcode in Settings → Privacy. The check is done by iOS; Auralis never sees your face or passcode. When the lock is on, the app is covered in the app switcher, your journal is removed from Spotlight, Siri won't read it, and the morning dream reminder doesn't show the intention you set for your dreams. A session is never interrupted by the lock, but if the app was away longer than a minute during it (an overnight Sleep session), it asks again when the session ends.

You can delete individual sessions (History) and journal entries (Journal), delete all journal entries (Settings → Journal), or delete everything by deleting the app. A session or entry you delete moves to Recently Deleted (the Journal's More menu, or Settings → Data & Storage), where you can restore it or delete it right away; after 30 days it is erased. Like other apps, this data may be included in your own iCloud or computer backup of your iPhone, under Apple's terms.

Sharing an entry or letter. Sharing an entry or a Looking Back letter as an image or PDF makes that file on your device, from that entry or letter only, and hands it to the share sheet; where it goes is up to you.

Microphone and speech

When a session is running and listening is on, Auralis uses the microphone to transcribe your speech on your device, using Apple's speech recognition. Audio is processed in memory and discarded. It is never recorded, saved or sent anywhere. You can turn listening off at any time during a session.

Toning. In the Toning meditation, the same microphone audio is also analysed on your device to find the pitch you're singing, so Auralis can hold a drone at that note. Only the current note is kept, in memory, while the session runs; no audio is recorded or sent, and sung vowels are not sent to any AI model.

Automatic speech. In Journaling's Automatic speech, what you said is saved to your journal word for word (it syncs like any entry if iCloud sync is on), and at the end that transcript is sent once to the AI provider chosen for the session, to pick a few lines to read back.

Voice memos. Outside sessions, when you tap Voice memo in a journal entry, Auralis asks for the microphone (the first time) and records until you tap stop. The memo is saved with that entry only, as described above, and never sent to any AI model.

Song recognition

If you keep music from another app playing during a session (for example YouTube or Spotify), Auralis can recognize the song using Apple's ShazamKit. A few seconds of microphone audio are turned on your device into a signature (a fingerprint of the music that can't be turned back into sound or speech), and only that signature is sent to Apple's Shazam service to find the song. Auralis doesn't listen for songs while it is speaking. Recognition is handled by Apple under Apple's privacy policy.

Artificial intelligence

Auralis generates replies in one of these ways, depending on your settings:

  1. On-device (Apple Intelligence): on supported iPhones, replies are generated on your device. Nothing is sent anywhere. On devices without Apple Intelligence, you can choose in Settings to download a private open model (Qwen3 1.7B or 4B on a phone, about 1 to 2.3 GB, from Hugging Face). The download contacts Hugging Face's servers for the model files only; no personal data is sent. Once downloaded, replies are generated on your device and nothing is sent anywhere. You can remove the model in Settings. Natural voices: you can also download an open voice model (Kokoro, about 330 MB, from Hugging Face) in Settings → Voice. The download fetches the model files only; the voice is then made on your device and nothing you say or hear is sent anywhere. You can remove it in Settings. Your Mac: if you turn on sharing in Auralis on your Mac, your iPhone and iPad can use the Mac's larger downloaded model, at home or away. The devices connect directly to each other over an encrypted peer-to-peer connection (WebRTC); requests and replies go only between your devices. To set up the connection, each device writes its network addresses and a signed offer or answer to your private iCloud database, and asks public STUN servers (Google, Cloudflare) for its own public address; no conversation text passes through iCloud or any server. A shared secret in iCloud Keychain makes sure only your own devices can connect. On the Mac, a larger model can run in Auralis Model Host, a companion Mac app, which shares it with your iPhone and iPad the same way (your private iCloud for setup, the same iCloud Keychain secret, a direct encrypted connection); Auralis on the Mac can also pass it requests over the Mac's own loopback address, which nothing outside the Mac can reach.
  2. Private Cloud Compute: not used. Auralis will offer it, off by default, once Apple approves it for the app; then requests may be processed by Apple's Private Cloud Compute, which Apple designed so your data is not retained or accessible to Apple.
  3. External AI provider (optional): if you add your own API key for a provider (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google Gemini, or a server you set up), the text of the current conversation, and, in Journaling, Custom, Record a Dream, Dreamwork, Active Imagination and Arcana Reading sessions, a few passages from your past sessions or journal that are relevant to what you just said, is sent to that provider to generate a reply. Now and then a Journaling, Custom, Inquiry or Dreamwork session opens by picking up something from your last two weeks (Settings → Journal → "Pick up where we left off", on unless you turn it off): the one sentence you wrote or said about it, or a dream image, goes to that provider to word the opening line (with no key, your Mac's model words it, or a set line is used and nothing is sent). Which ones were picked up is kept on this device. If you've added keys for more than one and the one you chose is unavailable, Auralis may use another one you added. If you've turned on any traditions (Settings → Traditions), their names and a sentence about each go with the request (replies, Looking Back, the briefing for readings), so they can use their terms. In Dreamwork, Active Imagination, Arcana, Integration, Learning and a tradition's practices, a few short passages from the Library's own courses (Auralis's text, chosen on your device by matching what you said) go with the request too; nothing of yours is added to them. Tradition reminders are scheduled on your device; nothing about them is sent anywhere. When you ask a question about your past sessions or journal (in Search, with Ask Auralis on Today, the Journal, Siri, Control Center or the widget, or by voice in a session), the question and the passages that match it are sent to check the matches and write the answer; nothing else from your journal goes with them. The questions offered under "Ask about your journal" are written once a day, or when the journal changes, by the same AI that answers it (your own devices when replies are Private), from short excerpts of your last entries and the themes, dream images and names Patterns found. A question about sleep, heart or rest is answered with Apple Health too: when your own Mac's model or a model on your device writes the answer, it's given your last four weeks of nightly sleep hours, resting heart rate and heart rate variability, and the patterns Auralis found. A cloud AI service is never given those numbers; it gets only the pattern sentences, and only if you turn on Include health patterns with cloud AI. Siri and Shortcuts open the answer only on an unlocked device, and the app's own Face ID lock still applies. When you ask Practice to make a path from your journal, the theme you chose and up to six entries about it are sent to write the days. The title and artist of the song playing are included so replies can refer to it, and the local time of day, so a question about the time can be answered. In the Journal, features you start yourself send the entries they work on: reflecting on an entry sends that entry and up to three earlier ones; a symbol's thread sends the entries that mention it; Looking Back sends every entry in the span you chose (a week, a month or three months); adding a session to your journal (and, when it ends, a Record a Dream or Integration session, unless you turned journal entries after sessions off) sends what you said in it (or, if you said little, its notes) to be made into an entry in your own words; proofreading an entry sends that entry. To tell dreams from other entries, Auralis also sends the opening lines of each entry once. That data is handled under the provider's own terms and privacy policy. In Storytelling and Learning, the topic you give is sent too; if you choose to continue a dream as a story, that dream's text is sent as the topic. In Dreamwork, the dream you choose is sent so the replies can work with it. Auralis never sends audio.
  4. Built-in responses: without any of the above, Auralis uses fixed, on-device responses and sends nothing.

Which one answers. With AI Provider set to Private, only the on-device models (1) answer, and nothing goes to a service. With External, only your provider (3) answers. With Automatic, Auralis uses the best available: your provider (3) first, if you've added a key and are online, then your Mac's model, then Apple's on-device model, then this device's downloaded model, then built-in responses. So with Automatic and a key, conversations are sent to your provider whenever you're online; choose Private to keep them on your devices.

Safety check. When something you say in a session sounds heavy (a few words checked on your device first), the same model that answers is also asked whether it might be a wish not to exist: it gets that sentence and the two lines before it, nothing else. Its one-word answer can bring up Need Help; it is kept only while the session runs and is never saved or included in exports or problem reports.

Auralis Cloud (optional subscription). If you subscribe to Auralis Cloud and it is the service in use (replies Automatic or Cloud), the request a reply needs — the same text an external provider would receive — goes over HTTPS to Auralis's relay, a small program on Cloudflare Workers, which passes it to the company that runs the open model (now Together AI, running DeepSeek V4.1 Flash for replies, and a larger open model such as DeepSeek V4 Pro for reading across your whole journal, up to 30 times a day; named in Settings → AI & Models → Auralis Cloud) and streams the answer back. If that company's service is down or too slow, the relay asks a backup instead: an open model run by Cloudflare Workers AI on Cloudflare's network (the same company that runs the relay), which receives the same request (shortened to fit that model if it's very long) and keeps neither it nor the answer. With the request, the app sends the App Store's signed record of your subscription; the relay asks Apple whether it is active and never learns your name or Apple Account. The relay does not log or store what you say or what the model answers, nor the model service's error messages: it keeps only a flag saying the subscription is active (for an hour) and counts of today's use (requests, journal reads, pictures, characters spoken; for up to two days), both filed under a one-way hash of the subscription's id. It also keeps anonymous daily totals for running the service (how many replies, voice lines, pictures and journal reads were asked for that day, how many failed and with which status, and which model answered, and what that used: tokens, characters and pictures, and their estimated cost), with nothing in them about who asked or what was said; they're kept 120 days. To spot unusually heavy use, a subscription's estimated cost for the day is kept with its other counts under the same hash, and a hash whose day passes another 25 cents is listed with that figure for eight days; nothing else about you goes with it. When something fails, the developer gets an alert with counts, status codes and model names only. Apple also tells the relay when a subscription is refunded, renewed or lapses (its signed notice is checked as the app's is), so the "active" flag is updated at once; only the notice's type is logged. The developer is told that someone subscribed or a subscription ended, and gets a daily line of counts (requests, failures, estimated cost, subscriptions started and ended), never who. The model service is used under a contract term that it keeps no prompts or answers and trains on none. If your sessions use an Auralis Cloud voice, each line Auralis is about to say is sent the same way, through the relay to the same company's open voice model (Kokoro), with the voice you chose (if that voice is down, Cloudflare Workers AI's voice model speaks the line instead, on Cloudflare's network); the audio comes back and is kept only in this device's cache. The relay keeps a count of characters spoken today, under the same hash, and nothing else. Cancelling the subscription stops all of this; Private keeps everything on your devices.

Paths that remember. A day of a path builds on the days before: the short summaries of the sessions that completed its earlier days go with that day's session to the model that answers, and to the same model once while you set it up, to write a line linking them to today. Nothing else from those sessions is sent.

Photos of tarot cards (Arcana Reading) If you photograph your cards or choose a photo of them for Arcana Reading, Auralis reads the card names on your device with Apple's text recognition and keeps only which cards you drew. Your photo is compared with the reference cards on your device; it isn't stored or sent anywhere. The cards, their traditional meanings and your question are part of the reading, sent to an AI provider only as described above. So the reading can be about your life, it also includes, while "Journal informs sessions" is on in Settings: a few lines from each of your last several journal entries and recent dreams (from the past three weeks), your journal's recurring themes this month, your last session's summary and your path day, and a private portrait of your life (the same, without the portrait, when you ask setup to suggest questions): a briefing on the experiences, people, places, ideas and dreams in your journal, which a model writes from your whole journal when you first set up a reading and then updates with your new entries. The portrait is kept on your device. Writing or updating it sends your journal (or its new entries) to the model doing the writing: your AI provider's strongest model if you've added a key and chose Automatic or External, otherwise your Mac's model. It is never written by a model on this device, which isn't accurate enough for it. A second pass by the same model checks the portrait against your journal, line by line, and removes anything the journal doesn't support (so your journal goes to that model twice). Each line quotes the words of the entry it comes from; your device then drops any line whose words aren't found in your journal (this check stays on the device). The same goes for Looking Back's letters, the threads of a symbol or a dream series, reading your dreams for series, and writing a personal path. Without a key or your Mac, those wait until one is available.

The Library (an optional tab) keeps its courses and your progress on your device, synced through iCloud with your settings when sync is on. When you add a topic, what you typed and the names of the traditions you've turned on go to the same model (the strongest hosted model with your key, else your Mac's) to write its course. "Suggest more for me" sends your traditions and the themes of your sessions and journal (single words, not your entries). Going deeper into a finished course, writing a tradition's course afresh, or writing a part out to read sends the course's outline (not your journal) to that model. A Library course or story is taught in a Learning or Storytelling session like any other. Asking about a lesson, recall questions and fact-checking a written course send that course's text (not your journal) to the same model. "Where this shows up for you" sends a few journal entries that touch the part (only those) to the model your "Where replies come from" setting chooses: with Automatic, your AI provider (3) if you've added a key and are online, else your Mac's model or this device's; with Private, only your own devices. A topic or path without an icon of its own gets one drawn by OpenAI's image model when you've added an OpenAI key, or else by Auralis Cloud's open image model when you subscribe: its title and a line about it are sent, nothing else. The Library's feed is put together on your device. Posts written for it are written from the courses' text (never your journal) by the model your replies come from (with Automatic, your hosted provider and key while online, then your Mac's model, then this device's; with Private, only your own devices), and kept on your device. What you linger on, save, open and mark "More" or "Less like this" is kept only on this device to order the feed, and is never sent anywhere. The feed can also show your own practice from this device: a session from about a week ago (what it was, how long, its summary's line), a path's next day, passages you kept in the Library with your notes, and open questions Looking Back drew from your journal; these are read on the device to put the feed together and are not sent anywhere. With an OpenAI key or an Auralis Cloud subscription and "Pictures drawn for posts" on, a few posts a day get a picture drawn (by OpenAI's image model with your key, else by Auralis Cloud's open one): only that post's short title and first words are sent (never a recall question, never anything from your journal), and the picture is kept on your device. "A lesson each morning" is a notification scheduled on your device with a line from a course you follow; nothing is sent anywhere. Sharing a topic makes a link you send yourself: a built-in topic's link carries only its name, and one written for you carries its course outline, glossary, texts and stories (not your progress, your journal or the lessons written out for you).

The same portrait (shortened for a model on your own devices) also goes with each request in conversation sessions (Journaling, Dream sessions, Active Imagination, Inquiry, Custom, and Psychedelic Support when exploring ideas), so Auralis understands who you are; with a key, starting one of those sessions may update it in the background. Turn "Journal informs sessions" off to keep all of this out.

OpenAI voices and journal images (optional)

If you add an OpenAI API key and choose an OpenAI voice, the text Auralis is about to speak is sent to OpenAI to turn it into speech: Auralis's own replies and guidance, and any text you ask it to read aloud, such as a journal entry or text you share to Auralis from another app. Your voice is never sent.

If you ask Auralis to make an image for a journal entry, the entry's text is sent to your AI provider (above) to describe a scene, and only that scene description is sent to paint the picture: to OpenAI with your key, or, without one (or when its credits run out), to Auralis Cloud, whose relay passes it to an image model run by Cloudflare Workers AI on Cloudflare's network, the same company that runs the relay (Leonardo's Lucid Origin or Phoenix, or the open FLUX.1 [schnell]; Together AI only if those fail) and keeps neither the description nor the picture; a subscriber can make up to 20 pictures a day. The image is stored with the entry on your device.

Both are handled under OpenAI's terms and privacy policy.

Beta feedback (TestFlight only)

In test builds, Auralis may ask after a session how it went. If you choose to send feedback, it goes by email from your own Mail app (or the share sheet), and you see it before it's sent. It contains your note, the app build and device model, and, only if you leave Include the transcript and logs on, that session's setup, transcript, timeline and technical logs. We use it only to fix problems, and delete it on request. Your answer (worked well, or something was off) is also kept with the session, on your device and in your iCloud like the session itself, never your note. The App Store version doesn't ask.

Reporting a problem

Settings → About → Contact Support, and Report a Problem in each session's review, let you email the developer a question, a problem or feedback. It goes from your own Mail app (or as a file you share), and nothing is sent until you tap Send. It contains what you write, an email address if you add one, the app version and your device model. Only if you turn on Include diagnostics (offered for a problem), which you can read in full first, it also contains the AI setting and the models that answered in that session, the session's timeline of events (their kinds, timings, states and model names, never what you said, Auralis's replies or your journal), and the app's audio, voice, sync and Live Activity logs from around it, which record states and counts only, and the reports iOS makes for the app about once a day (MetricKit: hangs, crashes, slow launches and where in the app's code they happened; kept only on your device until you include them). We use it only to fix the problem, and delete it on request.

Auralis Cloud's page also asks the relay how much of today's allowance you've used. That request carries only your subscription's signed transaction; the relay answers from the counts it already keeps for its daily limits and stores nothing new.

Sharing a summary (optional)

Journal → ⋯ → Share a summary (or Settings → Journal & Reminders) makes a summary for a clinician, therapist or guide: the sessions and entries you choose, as a PDF and a plain-text copy. It's made on your device and goes only where you send it through the share sheet; we never receive it. Sessions appear as facts (when, what, how long, your check-ins); entries in your own words. Your saved AI reflections and your health patterns are included only if you turn them on. With the app lock on, Face ID (or your passcode) is asked first.

Reminders that follow your rhythms (optional, off by default)

A reminder to integrate the afternoon after a Psychedelic Support session (only if you ask in its review), a bedtime reminder at the time you usually start Sleep (worked out on your device from your Sleep sessions), and a gentle reminder when a path or course has been quiet for three days. They are notifications scheduled on your device; nothing is sent anywhere. A path made from your journal is never named in a reminder.

Stories from your own imagery (optional)

In Storytelling, "Draw on my journal and dreams" gives the storyteller up to six single words that recur in your journal and dreams (such as "river" or "owl"), found on your device. Only those words go to the model telling the story (the one your AI setting chooses), never what you wrote, and the names of people only if you turn that on.

Apple Health (optional, off by default)

If you turn on Log to Apple Health in Settings, Auralis writes two kinds of entries to the Health app on your iPhone: Mindful Minutes for finished Meditation, Breathwork, Shamanic Journey, Album Journey and Inquiry sessions, and State of Mind from your arriving and leaving check-ins.

Separately, only if you turn on Show sleep and heart patterns, Auralis reads your sleep, resting heart rate, heart rate variability and the mindful minutes other apps logged, and works out on your iPhone how they go with your sessions, your check-ins, your journal's themes and the dreams you record (for example, how you slept on nights after an evening session). The patterns are shown on Today and in Noticing. Your Health data itself is never sent to a cloud AI service, to iCloud or to us; the only model that ever sees its numbers is your own Mac's (over Auralis's link) or one on your device, when you ask about sleep or heart.

A Looking Back letter may mention a few of those patterns. When it's written by your own Mac's model (over Auralis's link) or a model on your device, it's given the patterns as a few sentences of findings (like "in weeks with three or more sessions you slept 25 minutes longer a night on average"), never the Health data. When a cloud AI service could write it, the patterns are left out unless you turn on Include health patterns with cloud AI (off by default); with it on, those sentences go with the letter's request to the service you chose, under its terms. You can revoke or change any of this at any time in the Health app or in Auralis's settings.

After a Sleep session, the session shows how long it took you to fall asleep: Auralis asks once (with Apple's Health sheet) to read sleep, and finds when the night's sleep began after the session started. This is read on your iPhone in the same way, kept only on your iPhone (not in iCloud), and never leaves it.

Apple Watch. If you have the Auralis Watch app, it follows a session started on your iPhone: it runs a mindfulness workout to read your heart rate, shows it, and sends it to the iPhone, where the session can ease the music toward calm when your heart rate climbs. The workout isn't saved to Health. About once a minute a reading is kept for the session's review, on that iPhone only: it isn't part of the session's record, so it never goes to iCloud, even with sync on, and is left out of device backups (the session's log notes only that a reading came, without the number). It's never sent to an AI provider. You can turn the Watch off for sessions, or just the music easing, in Settings → Health & Devices.

The Watch app also has breathing sessions of its own. The first time you start one, the Watch asks (with Apple's Health sheet) to run a mindfulness workout, read heart rate and save Mindful Minutes. During the session the workout keeps it running with your wrist down and shows your heart rate on the Watch; the workout is then discarded, and if you allowed it the session is saved to Health as Mindful Minutes (its start and end time, nothing else). The heart rate stays on the Watch and isn't kept. The Watch keeps a count of today's breathing minutes for its own screen and complications.

Your iPhone and Watch talk to each other directly over Apple's WatchConnectivity (the same private link Apple uses between paired devices; Auralis has no server in between). The iPhone sends the Watch the running session's name, part and time left, and, for the Watch's home screen and complications, today's path day (the path's name and the day's title), the day's quotation from the Library, and today's practice minutes. The Watch sends the iPhone your heart rate during a session, and your requests to start a Meditation, Sleep, Breathwork or Integration session there or to pause, resume or end the running one. None of it goes anywhere else.

The iPhone also sends the Watch the timing of the next minute or so of breaths (so the Watch can tap them), your usual bedtime and whether yesterday was a Psychedelic Support session (so the Smart Stack can offer Sleep or Integration at the right time). From the Watch you can:

  • Record a dream. What you dictate goes to your iPhone and is saved in your journal as a dream, like a dream written in the morning notification. The Watch keeps the first few words of your last ten dreams, to show whether each has reached your journal.
  • Check in at the start or end of a session. Your calm and energy are saved with that session and, if you allowed it, logged to Health as State of Mind, as on the iPhone.
  • Ask Auralis. Your question goes to your iPhone, which answers it from your journal and sessions with the AI you chose, exactly as Ask Auralis does on the iPhone (see the AI section), and sends the answer back. While App Lock is on, the iPhone doesn't answer.

Heart rate and stillness guiding a session (both in Settings → Health & Devices). "Heart rate guides the session" is off unless you turn it on: in Breathwork your heart rate can gently slow the breath, and in Psychedelic Support a sharp, lasting rise can bring an offer to come back to the breath. "Notice when I fall asleep" (on by default) uses your heart rate and, during a Sleep session only, how still your wrist is (the Watch's motion sensor; it asks permission first) to fade the session early when you seem asleep, and keeps that time on your iPhone (not in iCloud) so the session's review can show it. The motion readings are reduced on the Watch to a single "how still" number every half minute, sent to your iPhone and not kept. Heart rate and motion stay on your Watch and iPhone and are never sent to an AI provider.

Music

If you connect Apple Music, Auralis uses Apple's MusicKit to play music and read your library or playlists for soundtrack choices. Playback and your Apple Music account are handled by Apple under Apple's privacy policy. Auralis does not share your sessions or journal with Apple Music. If you keep music from another app playing, Auralis reads only what the system reports as playing (title and artist), and can pause or resume it when you ask.

Shared paths (optional)

You can do a path with friends, each on your own phone. When you start a group, Auralis keeps it in your own iCloud and shares it, through Apple's iCloud sharing, only with the people you invite; we run no server and see none of it. Everyone in the group sees: the path, each member's chosen name (up to 30 characters), which days each member has completed and when. There are no messages between members. Nothing from your sessions or your journal is ever shared, nor what you said, how you felt, or how long you practiced. iCloud's sharing screen may also show participants' Apple Account names, as it does for any shared item. Safety features
